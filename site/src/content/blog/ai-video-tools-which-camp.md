AI video tools split into two camps, and picking the wrong camp costs you a week. **Avatar tools** (talking head, script in, presenter out) are for explainer and training content. **Generative tools** (prompt in, footage out) are for B-roll and stylised sequences. Almost every disappointed buyer bought one expecting the other.

## Which camp do you actually need

If your script has a person talking to camera for more than 30 seconds, you need an avatar tool. If your script is narration over visuals, you need a generative tool plus a decent stock library.

## The pricing trap nobody mentions

Minutes-based pricing looks generous until you count retakes. A 3-minute finished video usually costs 9-12 minutes of render credit once you factor in the two versions you throw away.

| Use case | Camp | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course lessons | Avatar | Lip-sync drift on long takes |
| Product B-roll | Generative | Inconsistent objects between shots |
| Faceless YouTube | Generative + TTS | Clip length caps |

## What breaks at scale

At one video a week nothing breaks. At 40 clips a month, queue times and per-seat pricing decide the winner, not output quality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use AI video commercially?
Check the specific plan. Commercial rights are usually tied to paid tiers, not the free tier.

### Do these replace an editor?
No. They replace the shooting stage, not the edit.
