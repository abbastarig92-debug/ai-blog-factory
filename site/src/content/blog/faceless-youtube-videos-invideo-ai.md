## Phase 1: Structuring the Prompt and Script for Maximum Retention

The opening moments often determine whether a viewer stays or clicks away. When left unprompted, InVideo AI defaults to standard introductory narration: *"Have you ever wondered about the mysteries of deep space? Today, we will explore..."* This style can weaken viewer retention.

Frame your generation prompt around an immediate visual and psychological hook. Instruct the engine to start mid-action or lead with a counter-intuitive statement. 

```text
Create a YouTube video documentary on the economics of commercial aviation. 
Target Audience: Aviation enthusiasts and business analysts.
Tone: Investigative, analytical, fast-paced.
Hook directive: Do not start with a greeting or rhetorical question. Open on a striking industry contradiction immediately.
Pacing: Fast visual cuts. Avoid corporate meeting B-roll.
```

### Pre-Written Scripts vs. AI Generation
Avoid letting InVideo write long-form documentary scripts entirely from scratch. While external tools can draft outlines, writing or heavily editing your own script helps support original research and reduce algorithmic repetition. InVideo AI functions best when you select the "Script to Video" workflow and paste your polished narrative text directly into the prompt interface. 

To maintain pacing across a full-length video:
* Insert line breaks regularly to force scene splits.
* Place explicit tone directives in brackets (e.g., `[whispering cadence]`, `[abrupt silence]`).
* Keep script paragraphs brief to prevent the AI from generating extended static video clips over a single continuous thought.

## Phase 2: Dialing In Voiceover, Cadence, and Audio Beds

A flat, synthetic voiceover can signal low-effort automation. InVideo AI includes native text-to-speech models, but using them without adjustment can create a noticeable synthetic drag. 

Select voices with natural vocal inflections and regional variation. If a voice reads too slowly, use the pacing modifier to increase the speed slightly. This shift helps remove unnatural pauses between sentences and keeps the audio moving at a modern video cadence.

```
[Target Audio Balance]
Voiceover Track:       Crisp, centered, dominant
Background Music Bed:  Audible underneath speech
Sound Effects (SFX):   Accenting cuts and text pop-ins
```

### Audio Ducking and Separation
Background music tracks generated inside InVideo can compete with the mid-range frequencies of AI voices. Access the audio panel on the timeline and lower the background music bed relative to the narration. Ensure auto-ducking is enabled so the music drops dynamically whenever the narrator speaks.

### Uploading External Voice Assets
If native voice profiles sound too rigid, bypass them entirely. External tools like ElevenLabs offer voice tiers starting at $5/month, with options including Creator ($11/month) and Pro ($99/month). 

Export your script's audio from ElevenLabs as an audio file. Upload that file into InVideo's timeline using the "Uploads" tab, align the narration track, and instruct InVideo to snap the B-roll cuts directly to your imported audio.

## Phase 3: Curating and Replacing B-Roll to Eliminate Stock Slop

Default AI video generation often leans on literal visual matches: if your script mentions "growing personal wealth," InVideo might populate the scene with a shot of a person smiling at a tablet or a plant growing out of coins. 

Use the **Edit Media** panel to systematically replace weak visuals:

1. Click on the scene block on your visual timeline.
2. Select **Edit Media**.
3. Choose the search filter and toggle on premium integrations (iStock and Storyblocks).
4. Search for contextual metaphors rather than literal phrases. If the script discusses "economic decline," search for *factory shut down, empty shipping containers, rain on glass,* or *abandoned construction.*

```
Script Line: "The company ran out of cash within months."
Avoid:       A sad person staring at an empty wallet.
Search For:  Timelapse trading floor red screens, server room cooling off, auction gavel falling.
```

### Motion Effects on Static Imagery
When stock footage fails to convey an abstract concept, incorporate custom AI images, archival photographs, or infographics. To prevent static images from stalling your video's visual momentum:
* Apply slow directional zooms drifting in or out smoothly.
* Split a continuous sentence across distinct visual angles of the same subject.
* Use InVideo's trim tool to clip unnecessary heads and tails off stock videos, keeping movement focused on peak action.

## Phase 4: Styling Captions, Motion Typography, and Branding

Subtitles help maintain engagement on mobile feeds, but basic formatting can look generic. Customize subtitle styling rather than relying on default presets.

### Formatting Subtitle Layers
Open the subtitle styling panel to customize typography:
* **Font selection**: Choose high-weight, condensed sans-serif fonts such as Montserrat Bold, Anton, or League Spartan.
* **Text sizing**: Keep subtitles legible on a mobile display without obstructing key parts of the frame.
* **Highlighting**: Use active word-by-word color highlights (such as bright yellow or neon green) against neutral base text.

### Mobile UI Safe Zones
YouTube's mobile interface overlays UI elements directly across the lower third and sides of vertical Shorts and the lower border of horizontal videos:

```
+---------------------------------------------+
|              TOP SAFE AREA                  |
|                                             |
|   [ Optimal Subtitle / Title Zone ]         |
|                                             |
|                                             |
|   [ Mobile Overlay Zone - Avoid Captions ]  |
+---------------------------------------------+
```

Position lower thirds and dynamic captions safely above the bottom border in vertical (9:16) canvases, and safely centered in horizontal layouts.

Save your customized text styles as a default preset. Applying consistent subtitle fonts, accent colors, and transition animations across your timeline establishes recognizable visual branding for your channel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you monetize faceless videos created with InVideo AI?
Yes, provided the output is substantially edited. Automated channels that upload unedited AI content risk demonetization under reused and repetitive content guidelines. To protect monetization eligibility, write original scripts, replace generic stock clips with relevant visuals, adjust pacing, and add distinct commentary.

### How do you stop InVideo AI from picking irrelevant or repetitive stock footage?
Avoid vague prompts. Provide clear visual instructions in your generation brief, including instructions on what to avoid. Once the rough cut generates, open the "Edit Media" tab and replace repetitive clips by searching InVideo's integrated libraries for narrative metaphors rather than literal script keywords.

### Should you write your own script or let InVideo AI generate it?
Writing your own script or heavily editing an outline prior to production is generally recommended. When AI generates scripts from minimal prompts, it can default to predictable structures and repetitive transition phrasing. Writing your own script helps ensure original research and better narrative pacing.

### What is the best way to handle AI voiceovers without sounding robotic?
Speed up native voices slightly to smooth out unnatural pauses, and lower the background music bed relative to the voiceover for vocal clarity. Alternatively, generate voice tracks using dedicated voice platforms like ElevenLabs and import the audio files directly into InVideo's timeline.

### How many InVideo generation credits does a standard YouTube video consume?
Credit consumption depends on your subscription tier and iteration volume. Generating multiple revisions can consume significant amounts of credits per project. To conserve limits on plans with fixed allowances, make manual adjustments on the timeline instead of re-generating the entire video from scratch.
