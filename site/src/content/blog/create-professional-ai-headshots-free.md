## Step 1: Preparing Your Source Photos for Maximum Realism

The final output of any AI face-swap or generation tool depends on the data you feed it. If you upload a low-resolution crop from a group photo, the AI will output plastic skin and asymmetric pupils. 

Follow these four rules when taking or selecting your source selfies:

*   **Soft, Natural Lighting:** Avoid direct sunlight or harsh overhead office bulbs. Stand near a window facing the light to get even illumination across your nose and cheeks.
*   **Neutral, Clean Backgrounds:** Stand in front of a solid, light-colored wall. Busy backgrounds confuse the AI during face extraction.
*   **Direct Gaze and Expression:** Look straight into the camera lens with a natural, slight smile. Do not wear sunglasses, hats, or heavy makeup.
*   **Varied Angles:** If your workflow requires multiple uploads, use slightly different head tilts. Keep your shoulders square to the camera.

Keep your camera at a comfortable distance from your face and use optical zoom to prevent the wide-angle distortion common in close-up phone selfies.

## Step 2: Generating Headshots with Open-Source AI (Flux.1 & Stable Diffusion)

You can access elite open-source models like `FLUX.1 [schnell]` on Hugging Face using their free Community tier. These models generate photorealistic skin textures, accurate hands, and realistic eye glints.

To start generating, open a free Flux.1 Schnell Space on Hugging Face. Set your aspect ratio to match standard portrait crops. Adjust the guidance scale for the Schnell model to maintain high prompt adherence without burning out the colors.

Copy and paste these production-tested templates into the prompt box:

### Corporate LinkedIn Prompt
> "A professional corporate headshot of a person, navy blue blazer, white collared shirt, neutral modern office background with soft bokeh, warm studio lighting, shot on 85mm lens, f/1.8, subtle skin pores, highly detailed, realistic eyes, professional photography."

### Creative & Tech-Casual Prompt

If you have a dedicated Nvidia GPU and sufficient system RAM, you can run Fooocus locally on your machine for enhanced privacy. Fooocus uses Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) under the hood and automates the complex background settings so you can generate professional portraits offline.

## Step 3: Using Freemium Web Apps for Instant Results

If you want a fast result without writing complex prompts, use daily free credits on web platforms like SeaArt or Remaker AI. These platforms specialize in face-swapping, which lets you bypass the need to generate a body from scratch.

Here is the exact face-swap workflow:

1.  **Generate or Find a Target Portrait:** Use a high-quality, AI-generated corporate portrait from Step 2 as your template.
2.  **Upload to the Face-Swap Tool:** Upload the target template portrait to the "Target Image" slot.
3.  **Upload Your Selfie:** Place your clear, front-facing source selfie into the "Source Image" slot.
4.  **Execute the Swap:** Run the generation. The tool will map your facial structure onto the professional clothing and lighting of the target.

To bypass watermarks and forced premium exports on freemium web apps, generate your images slightly wider than needed. You can then crop out the lower-right watermark using your phone's native photo editor. Alternatively, take a high-resolution screenshot of the generation preview screen before hitting the download wall.

## Step 4: Prompt Engineering for Natural Skin Texture and Studio Lighting

To make your AI headshots look real, you must eliminate the shiny, plastic look typical of cheap AI filters. Avoid buzzwords like "photorealistic," "8k," or "hyperrealistic." These words actually force the model to reference stylized digital art rather than real camera RAW files.

Instead, use specific photographic terms:

*   **For Skin Realism:** Add phrases like "subtle skin pores," "natural skin texture," "raw photography," "peach fuzz," and "soft skin details."
*   **For Studio Lighting:** Use "soft-focus modern boardroom," "warm window side-light," "85mm portrait lens," and "f/2.2 aperture."
*   **Negative Prompts (for SDXL and older models):** Use structured inputs like "deformed eyes, plastic skin, airbrushed, 3D render, glossy skin, asymmetric pupils, bad anatomy."

If you are using Flux, short and direct prompts work best. State the outfit, the background, and the lighting clearly in two or three sentences.

## Step 5: Free Post-Processing: Upscaling and Fixing AI Glitches

Even the best AI models occasionally output slightly blurry eyes or fuzzy hair edges. You can clean up these details using free post-processing tools.

First, download Upscayl, which is a free, open-source desktop application. It uses your computer's graphics card to upscale your headshot to high resolution without losing skin texture. 

If your upscaled image has minor artifacts, open the photo in Canva's free editor. You can use the basic retouching tools to lower the highlights on your forehead to reduce shiny spots. Adjust the contrast slightly and add a subtle cool or warm tint to mimic professional camera color science.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it truly possible to get professional AI headshots completely for free?
Yes. By using open-source models like Flux.1 Schnell on Hugging Face Spaces or running Fooocus locally on your computer, you can generate and upscale high-resolution, studio-quality portraits without spending money.

### How do I fix plastic skin, bad eyes, or weird hands in free AI generations?
Avoid using words like "hyperrealistic" in your prompts. Instead, describe camera settings like "85mm lens" and "subtle skin pores." If a generation has a minor glitch, run it through a free local upscaler like Upscayl to sharpen the details, or use Canva's free editor to adjust the contrast and smooth out artifacts.

### Which free AI tools don't train on my uploaded facial data?
Running open-source tools like Fooocus locally on your own computer helps ensure your photos remain offline. If you use web-based Hugging Face Spaces, choose private spaces or ephemeral templates that do not store your inputs.

### What are the exact prompts to get a corporate LinkedIn look versus a creative aesthetic?
For a corporate look, prompt: *"A professional corporate headshot of a person in a navy blue blazer, white shirt, neutral office background, soft studio lighting, 85mm lens, realistic skin texture."* For a creative look, prompt: *"A modern portrait of a person in a dark crewneck sweater, minimalist light studio background, warm window side-light, shallow depth of field."*
