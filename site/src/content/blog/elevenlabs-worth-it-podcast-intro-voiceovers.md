## Fine-Tuning for Impact: Stability and Clarity Settings

Default settings rarely yield punchy podcast hooks. You must adjust the voice lab sliders to dial in high-energy pacing.

The optimal approach for podcast intros involves carefully tuning the voice lab sliders. Lowering Stability injects natural vocal variations and pitch shifts, preventing monotone delivery. Raising Clarity sharpens consonant sounds without introducing digital harshness.

Increase the Style Exaggeration slider slightly if your intro script contains enthusiastic calls to action. To fix mispronounced show titles or brand names, use phonetic spelling (such as typing "SaaS" as "Sass" or "S-A-A-S") and generous dash punctuation to force realistic pauses.

## Speech-to-Speech: The Secret Weapon for Motion Designers

Text-to-Speech works for simple announcements, but struggles when you need exact audio timing for video intro graphics. Speech-to-Speech solves this problem by taking your spoken performance and replacing the vocal timbre with the AI voice.

By recording a quick scratch track into a basic microphone, you dictate the pitch, dramatic pauses, and cadence of the hook. The AI overlays its realistic voice model over your performance while preserving your original timing.

This workflow eliminates endless guesswork inside video editing software like Premiere Pro or After Effects. You can edit your motion graphics and kinetic typography directly to your scratch track, then substitute the AI audio cleanly over the final timeline.

## The Licensing Maze: Commercial Rights Explained

Licensing rules confuse many new podcasters. If you monetize your show or take on sponsors, you must select a plan that grants commercial rights.

The Free plan provides 10,000 monthly credits (roughly 10 minutes of generated audio), but strictly forbids commercial distribution and requires explicit credit attribution to ElevenLabs. To publish monetized intros on Spotify, Apple Podcasts, or YouTube, you must subscribe to at least the Starter plan ($5/month) or Creator plan ($11/month).

Regarding ownership: you own the audio files generated on paid plans for commercial use. However, you do not own exclusive rights to public library voices, which remain open for other subscribers to use on their own projects.
