## Evaluating AI Voices for TTRPGs: Accents, Creature Audio, and Batch Feasibility

Most AI voice generators are built for audiobooks and corporate presentations. They prioritize calm, midline cadences. When you push these engines to deliver a wheezing goblin or a roaring orc warboss, their neural vocoders interpret harsh rasps, screams, and guttural growls as audio artifacts and try to "clean" them out. 

ElevenLabs handles emotional performance well, but its credit system breaks down during extensive campaign prep. ElevenLabs API pricing sits at $50 to $100 per 1 million characters. A standard campaign module with numerous factions, background tavern barks, quest briefings, and dungeon encounter warnings can quickly accumulate a massive word count. When you regenerate takes to fix misplaced syllable emphasis on fantasy names like *Cthun* or *Silverymoon*, a single campaign prep session can burn through an entire $22 Creator or $99 Pro monthly allotment.

Batch production requires a pipeline that does not penalize trial and error. Game Masters need to pipe dialogue tables directly from Notion, Obsidian, or Google Sheets into an audio engine and export organized WAV or Ogg Vorbis files without monitoring a credit counter.

## In-Depth Reviews: The Best ElevenLabs Alternatives for Tabletop RPG Voice Acting

### 1. Fish Audio (Fish Audio S2 Pro)
Fish Audio focuses on creator-native expressive performance. In benchmark tests, the S2 Pro model scored an Elo rating of 1,128, rivaling ElevenLabs in perceived quality while remaining dramatically cheaper. It provides an open-weights model, allowing you to run it locally on consumer hardware or access it through a hosted API.

* **Performance & Range:** Excellent stability under extreme emotional prompts. It sustains stylized Scottish, Irish, and archaic cadences without drifting back into flat American cadences mid-sentence.
* **Pricing Math:** API access runs at $15 per 1 million characters.
* **The Catch:** The web interface is less refined than ElevenLabs. You will occasionally get erratic pacing on sentences packed with invented fantasy names unless you phonetically spell them out in your text prompt.

### 2. Cartesia (Sonic 3)
If you want live, interactive NPC responses inside your virtual tabletop, Cartesia is built specifically for low latency. Sonic 3 hits roughly 40ms time-to-first-audio (TTFA), leaving ElevenLabs' higher response times behind. 

* **Performance & Range:** Cartesia handles rapid dialogue delivery cleanly. It is tuned for responsiveness, making it ideal if you run voice-agent bots in Discord or local browser overlays. It supports 42 languages, giving you ample headroom for foreign-accented humanoids.
* **Pricing Math:** Cartesia starts with a free tier of 10,000 monthly credits. Paid access starts at $5 per month for 100,000 credits (Pro), moving to $49 per month for 1.25 million credits (Startup), and $299 per month for 8 million credits (Scale).
* **The Catch:** Cartesia's vocal library sits lower on pure dramatic grit. Arena benchmarks place it at rank #10, meaning it trades cinematic vocal nuance for raw generation speed. Highly grotesque monster lines tend to smooth out into normal human cadences.

### 3. Kokoro
Kokoro is an open-source text-to-speech engine that runs locally on your workstation. For GMs running homebrew campaigns who refuse to pay recurring subscriptions for one-off NPC barks, Kokoro is a viable zero-cost local option.

* **Performance & Range:** Kokoro handles standard human voices well. Accents are consistent, though it lacks the out-of-the-box theatricality of Fish Audio. It does not natively growl or cackle, so creature voices require post-processing.
* **Pricing Math:** Free and open-source. There are no token limits, character caps, or rate limits beyond what your GPU or CPU can compute.
* **The Catch:** There is no hosted web dashboard for non-technical users. You must run it via local Python environments or community-built Docker containers.

### 4. Inworld AI (TTS-2)
Inworld AI designed its TTS-2 engine explicitly for games, dynamic character dialogue, and virtual worlds. Where general-purpose TTS tools deliver static audio files, Inworld is built to handle the contextual logic and interactive dialogue flow typical of TTRPG sessions.

* **Performance & Range:** Excellent at maintaining distinct character personalities across varied prompts. It handles emotional swings—from hushed tavern conspiracies to battlefield combat screams—without clipping the audio output.
* **Pricing Math:** Inworld TTS-2 lists at $25 per 1 million characters on-demand, scaling down to $5 per million characters at enterprise volume. This makes it significantly cheaper than ElevenLabs' API price.
* **The Catch:** The platform is heavily geared toward real-time game developers. Setting up simple offline batch exports requires navigating an interface optimized for real-time game engines rather than simple audio downloads.

### 5. GPT-SoVITS
GPT-SoVITS is an open-source, reference-led voice cloning engine that lets you clone a character using a short audio sample. If you have an audio clip of a raspy fantasy goblin or an ethereal fey queen from your own vocal recordings, GPT-SoVITS can replicate the inflection and acoustic space accurately.

* **Performance & Range:** The dynamic range matches whatever reference audio you feed it. If your reference sample contains guttural vocal fry, the output retains that fry across new dialogue lines.
* **Pricing Math:** Free and open-source.
* **The Catch:** It requires technical setup and a capable modern GPU to run inference at reasonable speeds. Vocal cloning quality drops sharply if the reference sample has background music or reverb.

### 6. Speechmatics
Speechmatics is an enterprise-grade engine optimized for scale, consistency, and clean generation. While rarely discussed in gaming circles, its generous free allotment makes it an exceptional tool for GMs who write walls of descriptive lore text.

* **Performance & Range:** Extremely consistent pronunciation and clear phonetic execution. It reads complex fantasy proper nouns with fewer hallucinations than consumer-focused platforms, though its delivery leans slightly more formal.
* **Pricing Math:** Offers 1 million characters free each month. Once you exceed that, billing is $0.011 per 1,000 characters—between 11 and 27 times cheaper than standard ElevenLabs plans.
* **The Catch:** It lacks built-in monster modifiers or extreme fantasy archetypes. You must rely on clear writing and external pitch modification to turn its outputs into threatening creatures.

## Creature and Monster Sound Generation: Overcoming the 'Too Human' Engine Problem

Few hosted AI voice generators produce a convincing bugbear, lich, or mind flayer straight out of the box. Commercial engines are designed to sound pleasant. When you push them toward guttural growls, their vocoders choke, generating warbling metallic artifacts.

An effective workflow combines an expressive alternative like Fish Audio or GPT-SoVITS with a three-node digital audio workstation (DAW) chain:

```
[Raw AI Audio Generation]
          │
          ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Pitch & Formant Shifter │  --> Shift pitch down; shift 
└──────────┬──────────────┘      formant down for bulk.
           │
           ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Ring Modulator / Drive  │  --> Add grit and growl resonance 
└──────────┬──────────────┘      for demonic or insectoid textures.
           │
           ▼
┌─────────────────────────┐
│ Stereo Delay & Verb     │  --> Slapback delay + damp room
└──────────┬──────────────┘      reverb to simulate dungeon acoustics.
           │
           ▼
[Export: Normalized Ogg Vorbis]
```

To create an undead wraith, feed an engine like Fish Audio a whispered prompt. Take the clean output, pitch-shift it down while leaving the formant high, and apply a subtle slapback delay. This avoids the sanitization filters that ElevenLabs applies to extreme volume outputs, giving you genuine monster acoustics without synthetic warble.

## Automating NPC Production: CSV-to-Audio Workflows for Campaign Prep

When prepping a module with numerous dialogue barks, generating lines one by one inside a browser interface wastes hours. Using an API like Fish Audio ($15/1M characters) or a local script with Kokoro allows you to convert a campaign spreadsheet into organized assets in minutes.

### 1. Build the Campaign Dialogue Sheet
Export your dialogue notes from Notion or Obsidian into a basic CSV file formatted with explicit metadata:

```csv
filename,voice_id,text
NPC_Dwarf_Guard_01,dwarf_m_04,"Halt! No passage through the mountain gate without the Thane's seal."
NPC_Goblin_Scout_02,goblin_raspy_01,"Shiny boots! Give 'em here, tall-thing, or we slice your hamstrings!"
NPC_Elf_Aran_01,fey_ethereal_02,"The forest remembers your trespass. Turn back before twilight falls."
```

### 2. Batch Execution and Normalization
Run a local Python script to read the CSV, query your chosen engine's API, and stream the resulting audio directly to disk. 

Ensure your script or DAW runs an automated loudness pass. Set your targets to standard dialogue levels with an appropriate true peak ceiling. This ensures your NPC voice lines sit cleanly above background dungeon ambience without blowing out players' headphones.

### 3. VTT Optimization
Export dialogue assets as **Ogg Vorbis (.ogg)** files at standard sample rates. 

* **Foundry VTT:** Place the files inside your local `worlds/[world_name]/audio/npc_barks/` folder. Use the Ambient Sound tool or link files directly to token macro triggers to fire dialogue when players click on an NPC token.
* **Roll20:** Upload the .ogg files directly to the Roll20 Jukebox. Tag files with the character name (`Dwarf_Guard`) so you can quickly trigger them from your GM dashboard.

## Commercial Rights and Streaming Clearances for Actual Play Shows

If you publish an actual play podcast or stream on YouTube, Twitch, or Spotify, audio licensing matters. Platforms actively flag synthetic voice tracks that use unlicensed vocal likenesses.

* **ElevenLabs Licensing Terms:** ElevenLabs grants no commercial rights on its Free tier (10,000 characters/month). You must maintain an active paid subscription ($6 Starter, $22 Creator, or above) for commercial licensing.
* **Cartesia Licensing:** Cartesia includes commercial use on its $5/mo Pro tier and above, covering monetized video and podcast feeds.
* **Open-Source Freedom:** Kokoro and GPT-SoVITS give you total ownership of the raw generated audio, bypassing third-party terms of service, platform policy changes, or price spikes.
* **Actor Likeness Protection:** Avoid feeding unauthorized audio clips of living voice actors into cloning tools for an actual play broadcast; rely on pre-cleared platform voices, original recordings, or open-weights community reference models.
